# Language in Culture and Responsible NLP — Project 2

**Editorial gatekeeping in historical isiXhosa newspapers (1884–1888):**
a controlled comparison of the missionary-backed *Isigidimi sama-Xosa*
against J.T. Jabavu's independent *Imvo Zabantsundu*.

> **Research question.** Does editorial control show up in the lexical data?
> We measure per-page **political-term density** in 19th-century isiXhosa
> using a hand-built, theme-organised political lexicon, and compare the
> two newspapers with a full stack of robustness controls (OCR noise,
> document length, era drift, language purity).

## Reproduce the analysis end-to-end (3 commands)

```bash
# 1. Verify corpus integrity (read-only)
python -m scripts.check_data

# 2. Build issue-disjoint Train/Val/Test splits (writes results/splits/*.csv)
python -m src.data.loader \
  --xh-dir data/XhTxt \
  --en-dir data/EnTxt \
  --metadata data/LanguageSplitTracker70-94.csv \
  --out-dir results/splits

# 3. Score political density + run robustness controls
python -m src.interpret.political_scorer \
  --manifests results/splits/train_manifest.csv \
              results/splits/val_manifest.csv \
              results/splits/test_manifest.csv \
  --lexicon config/political_lexicon.yaml \
  --text-root data \
  --results-dir results
```

## Cleaning decisions (for the Method section)

The corpus required three cleaning steps before modelling:

1. **Drop empty files** — 1,061 one-sided empties (isiXhosa pages with a
   0-byte stub in `EnTxt/` and vice versa — artefacts of the language-split)
   plus 1 truly blank page. No content is lost; the content lives in the
   sibling directory.
2. **Join page-level metadata** — `LanguageSplitTracker70-94.csv` is keyed
   by filename (`IMVO_1884-11-03_p001.txt`), not by issue ID. All 6,153
   retained documents matched exactly (100.0% coverage).
3. **Filter for analysis**:
   - `language == "xh"` (the lexicon is isiXhosa-only)
   - `xh_pct >= 90` (exclude mixed-language pages where English political
     vocabulary would be invisible to the lexicon)
   - `n_tokens >= 200` (length floor; below this, density is unstable)

## Robustness controls (what they test, what they found)

| Control | Test | Result (final, filtered) | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCR (word length) | Spearman ρ between score and mean token length | **0.30** | Structural: isiXhosa political vocabulary is polysyllabic. Not an artefact — documented, not eliminated. |
| OCR (non-alpha ratio) | Spearman ρ between score and non-letter character ratio | **−0.36** | Structural: political content concentrates in clean prose pages, not in noisy tables. |
| Era | Spearman ρ between score and year | **−0.22** | Mild temporal trend; reported, not controlled-out. |
| Length | Spearman ρ between score and `n_words` | **0.05** | Clean — log-odds metric is length-invariant. |
| Language purity | Spearman ρ between score and `xh_pct` | **0.14** | Weak after `xh_pct >= 90` filter; residual 0.14 documented as a limitation. |
| **Source (IMVO vs ISIG)** | Mann–Whitney U on median log-odds | **U = 367,410, p = 1.5 × 10⁻⁵** | **Headline finding — see below.** |

---

## Headline result

After language-purity and length filtering (**n = 1,917 pages, 637 issues,
1884–1888 overlap**):

- **IMVO**: median political log-odds = **−0.058** (mean −0.087, std 0.313)
- **ISIG**: median political log-odds = **+0.006** (mean −0.028, std 0.397)
- Mann–Whitney U = **367,410**, **p = 1.48 × 10⁻⁵**

**Reading.** For the same isiXhosa token count, *Isigidimi* pages show
statistically higher political-term density than *Imvo* pages. The
qualitative sample corroborates this: sampled ISIG pages contain land-grant
tables, quit-rent records, and moral-governance commentary; sampled IMVO
pages contain front-page advertising, subscription notices, and commerce.

**Interpretation (cautious, not psychologising).** The missionary press
*lexically constructed* the isiXhosa political domain — the vocabulary of
government, land, and law — as part of its translation project. Jabavu's
independent, advertiser-funded paper produced a different **page
composition**: more commerce, more subscription notices, more community
news. The 0.06 log-odds gap reflects this editorial/compositional
difference, not a claim about intent or reader reception.

## Reproducibility notes for teammates

- **Do not** re-run `src.data.loader` with a different random seed unless
  you also regenerate all downstream files. The seed (`--seed 42`) is
  documented in the Method section.
- **Do not** edit `config/political_lexicon.yaml` without re-running the
  scorer **and** re-checking the controls CSV. The lexicon version is
  logged in the scoring header (currently `v0.2.0`, 116 terms, 7 themes).
- **Do not** commit `data/XhTxt/`, `data/EnTxt/`, or `venv/`. `.gitignore`
  is set up to prevent it; if you accidentally `git add` them, unstage with
  `git restore --staged <path>` before committing.
- The **manifests** in `results/splits/` are the reproducible hand-off
  point between the loader and the classifier/topic model
  and the scorer. If your `train_manifest.csv` has different
  `issue_id` values than the committed one, your corpus or your seed is
  different — stop and reconcile.
